Title: Balanced Multi-Perspective Checking of Process Conformance
Authors: Mannhardt, de Leoni, Reijers & van der Aalst
Published: Computing, 2016 (Open Access)

Research goal:
The goal of the paper is to check whether the real execution of a road traffic fine management process conforms to an expected process model and legal rules. Does the road traffic fine process follow the rules, the model, and the law?

Method:
They applied process conformance checking using a balanced multi-perspective approach, which considers:
- Control-flow: Are activities executed in the correct order?
- Data: Are values (e.g., dismissal codes, amounts) correct?
- Time: Are legal deadlines respected?
- Resources: Are actions performed by appropriate actors?
They used alignment-based conformance checking (in ProM) to compare:
- The event log (real executions)
- The process model (expected/legal behavior)
Research goal: propose a new conformance method

Results
From the road traffic fine dataset, they found:
- High overall fitness (average 0.96)
Many deviations still exist, such as:
- Incorrect dismissal codes
- Missed or late activities
- Legal deadline violations (e.g., Send Fine, Appeal handling)
A significant number of fines were:
- Sent too late
- Not forwarded to credit collection on time
These findings show non-compliance with legal and procedural requirements


limitations:
Focus on Conformance, Not Performance Analysis
This paper checks whether the process conforms to a model, but it does not analyze delays, waiting times, or bottlenecks in performance.
Your interest is on where and why delays/bottlenecks occur in the road traffic fine management process — a different task.

gap: the present research aims to investigate why these delays occur, which process stages constitute bottlenecks, and how these inefficiencies impact overall process performance.

PAPER 2
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10844-018-0507-6
Discovering more precise process models from event logs by filtering out chaotic activities

Problem: In real-world event logs (like those you’d get from business systems), some activities occur in a chaotic way — meaning they happen independently from the real control flow of the process (e.g., a user pressing a “Help” button at any time). These are not mistakes, but because they can appear anywhere, they confuse process discovery algorithms. Standard process discovery assumes that event ordering reflects true control flow. 

Key Insight: Chaotic activities distort directly-follows relations, which many mining algorithms use (like Inductive Miner). This leads to:

Process models that are too general

Models with a lot of artificial parallelism

Worse precision and interpretability 

Main Contribution:
The authors propose information-theoretic filtering methods to identify and remove chaotic activities from event logs before mining. They evaluate these methods on 17 real event logs and show that they find more precise and behaviorally specific models than frequency-based filters (which just remove low frequency activities).

limitations:
This paper only removes chaotic activities to improve model precision. It does not analyze where delays occur in the process, waiting times or bottlenecks, performance, cycle times, or delays between activities

That means the methods don’t quantify performance issues — which is essential if your focus is traffic fine management delays.

PAPER 3:
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0306437924000413
Enjoy the silence: Analysis of stochastic Petri nets with silent transitions

This paper develops a formal analytical framework for analyzing stochastic Petri nets that include silent transitions — i.e., transitions that occur in the model but have no observable activity in event logs (unlabeled internal steps). It does so in a way that:

Accounts for stochastic behaviour — capturing probabilistic variations and nondeterminism in process executions.

Connects Petri nets and finite execution traces — matching real event logs, where each case ends in a finite number of steps.

Handles silent transitions effectively — label mismatches where activities don’t clearly correspond to transitions.

Uses analytic methods (absorbing Markov chains + automata techniques) to compute probabilities and various analysis tasks.

Applies to process mining contexts — especially stochastic conformance checking and other probability-aware analyses.


LIMITATIONS:
- No Empirical Analysis of Performance / Delays
- Silent Transitions” Only Indirectly Linked to Real Queues
The main technical challenge they solve is modelling silent transitions (internal steps not visible in logs). But in many real-world systems, delays often occur because of visible waiting steps between activities (e.g., “await payment”, “review evidence”).

The paper doesn’t focus on how such waiting steps contribute to delays.
